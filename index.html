How ChatGPT answers a question

Large language models don't look answers up — they generate them one word at a time, each word chosen because it's statistically the most likely continuation given everything written so far. Ask something below and watch that word-by-word process happen, live.

How the real thing works

  • Training — the model reads enormous amounts of text and learns which words tend to follow which, across billions of examples.
  • Tokenizing — your question is broken into small chunks called tokens, the model's basic unit of text.
  • Prediction — at each step, the model calculates a probability for every possible next token and samples one.
  • Repeat — the chosen token is added to the sequence, and the process repeats until the answer is complete.

This demo builds a real, working version of that idea — a bigram model that learns word-to-word transitions from a small built-in corpus, then predicts one word at a time. It's the same mechanism at a tiny scale: real models predict from a vocabulary of tens of thousands of tokens, trained on essentially the whole internet.
